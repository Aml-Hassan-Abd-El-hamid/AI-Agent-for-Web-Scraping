Amal ‘Aziz and Dima Abu Shakayan

Amal ‘Aziz and Dima Abu Shakayan

( 19 October 2025 )
  • עברית
  • عربي
  • Facebook share
  • Twitter
  • WhatsApp

Amal ‘Aziz

A 39-year-old widow and mother of three, from Jabalya Refugee Camp. Amal described how her niece Dima was left alone and severely injured after the rest of her family were killed in an airstrike

When the war started, I stayed at my house in the al-‘Alami area of Jabalya Refugee Camp with my three children: Baker, 16, Hala, 14, and Asmaa, 11. My extended family lived in another part of Jabalya Refugee Camp. Like everyone in the Gaza Strip, I was shocked when rockets were fired toward Israeli towns on 7 October 2023. Right after that, the war broke out, and Gaza turned into a battlefield. Danger was everywhere. Every day, we heard about homes that had been bombed or that the military threatened to bomb.

On 20 October 2023, the military threatened to bomb the home of the al-‘Ajrami family, next to the building where my brothers and uncles lived. My brothers and their families left the building. My brother Amin’s family split up. His wife, Asmaa al-Halabi, 28, took their children, Dima, 11, Dana, 5, Tala, 4, ‘Abd a-Nur, two and a half, and Kinan, nine months, and went to her parents’ apartment in a three-story building not far from my brothers’ home. Amin stayed at his workplace. He worked as a guard at Kamal ‘Odwan Hospital.

On 23 October 2023, at around 2:00 A.M., I suddenly heard a massive explosion. I followed the news and, a few minutes later, I saw a report saying that the al-Halabi home had been bombed. I called my brother Amin immediately. He was at Kamal ‘Odwan Hospital and answered the phone crying. He told me they had brought his daughter, Tala, to the hospital with no signs of life.

Even though it was dangerous, I walked straight to Kamal ‘Odwan Hospital. I called my brothers on the way, told them about the strike, and they went to the al-Halabi home. They confirmed it was bombed and completely destroyed.

I stayed at the hospital with Amin while my other brothers went to the al-Halabi home to help recover the bodies. The bodies started arriving at the hospital, some dismembered. My sister-in-law Asmaa and my nieces and nephews Dana, Tala, ‘Abd a-Nur, and Kinan were among those killed. While recovering the bodies, the rescuers, including Civil Defense crews, heard Dima calling from under the rubble: “I’m here! I’m alive!” They worked for seven hours to get the debris off of her and get her out.

At the hospital, I tried to calm Amin, who was in terrible emotional distress. When they finally managed to pull Dima out and bring her to the hospital, she was in critical condition. They took her straight into surgery and then to the intensive care unit.

In addition to the family members, 50 IDPs had been staying in the al-Halabi building. Thirty of them were killed. Another 20 were injured, some severely.

After Amin’s wife and four of their children were buried, we transferred Dima to a-Shifaa Hospital for further treatment. She underwent several more operations there. She had fractures in her legs, burns, a head injury with burns, and more tendon and nerve lacerations.

On 25 October 2023, the Israeli military bombed the a-Taj building in the a-Rimal neighborhood, and many casualties were evacuated to the hospital, so they asked us to take Dima to free up space. We took her back to our house in Jabalya Refugee Camp. The trip back was very hard for her, because we had to carry her, and she was still in serious condition, in pain, terrified, and in shock.

Two days after we got home, I realized she needed to return to the hospital. I asked Kamal ‘Odwan Hospital to send an ambulance, and I went with her. They ran tests and found problems with her kidneys and liver. They started treatment, and she stayed in the hospital for 45 days. Her father, Ayman, and I stayed with her the whole time, while my children stayed at home. Then, the Israeli military invaded Jabalya and threatened to raid the hospital. We had no choice but to take Dima out and bring her back to my house.

The situation in our area became extremely hard. Many residents left, the streets were empty, and we were left isolated in our home. Dima was in pain. Her father and I took care of her constantly, and we also had to go out looking for food. I walked long distances just to find a can of tuna, a can of meat, or drinking water.

Later, we took Dima back to the hospital, and they removed the casts from her legs. I managed to find a physiotherapist to work with her, because I was afraid she would remain disabled. Because of Dima’s condition — she couldn’t walk due to her fractures and burns, and she needed diapers — we stayed in the house and couldn’t evacuate to the south. I also wanted to stay close to the hospital and the physiotherapist.

We stayed in this desperate situation for about six months, until things became even more dangerous and tanks got close to our home. On 2 May 2024, we had no choice but to flee. I left with my brother Amin and the kids. We put Dima on a small cart, and Baker pushed her. We walked all the way to a-Shati Refugee Camp, where we found shelter in some storage room. We are still living there, in extremely harsh conditions. We barely had any food or water. I did physiotherapy exercises with Dima myself. I talked to her constantly, trying to comfort and support her. She suffered from pain and anxiety and missed her mother and siblings terribly.

Whenever the situation allowed, Amin went back north to Kamal ‘Odwan Hospital to work. On 2 October 2024, he went there and met our brothers Muhammad and Ayman. He planned to work at the hospital for a week, and then bring Dima’s medical file, which had been left behind, and some clothes for her. He stayed at our brother Muhammad’s house, near mine.

On 9 October 2024, at around 1:00 P.M., the Israelis bombed the Abu Naser building in Jabalya Refugee Camp, next to my brothers’ building. Muhammad, Ayman, and Amin were near the building at the time, with my aunt Sanaa, my uncle’s wife, 48, her son ‘Abdallah, 20, and a few people from the Shbeir family. They were all collecting their things, preparing to evacuate south. But before they managed to leave, the building was bombed.

My brother Muhammad called me crying and told me that Amin was killed and that he and Ayman were injured and evacuated to the hospital. My aunt Sanaa and her son ‘Abdallah were also killed.

Dima took the devastating news of her father’s death very hard. She reacted hysterically, crying and hitting her legs until they bled.

Amin’s body stayed under the rubble until January 2025. Until then, my brothers and the neighbors removed debris by hand, a little each day, until they finally uncovered his body and brought him to burial. Now, everything Dima had in this world had been buried, her family, her memories, her dreams, and her hopes.

Dima is still suffering terribly because of the tendon and nerve damage. She can’t walk and depends on others for everything. She still receives physiotherapy and treatment for the wounds on her legs. She needs to undergo several surgeries, including plastic surgery and hair implants, because she lost hair from the burns on her head. She has already endured so much: her injuries, the loss of her entire family, painful treatments without anesthetics, displacement, and bombardments.

She needs treatments that are only available outside the Gaza Strip. It’s her only hope of going back to being a child like any other. She has lost so much and deserves a chance at rehabilitation. She is a smart, talented girl and was an excellent student. She loves writing poetry, and despite everything she has been through, she still has dreams.

Dima Abu Shakayan also spoke to B’Tselem field researcher:

When my grandfather’s house was bombed, I woke up from sleep to a loud noise and saw a red light, and then it disappeared. I couldn’t feel my body. I thought I was inside a dream. I tried to move, but I couldn’t. I was under the rubble.

Suddenly, a light appeared. I was hungry, thirsty, and terrified. I cried, “Help! Help me! Help me!” Then I heard someone trying to move the rubble off me, but I couldn’t see anyone. I tried to move but still couldn’t. A long time passed, and I was still under the rubble. It was dark, and I felt like I was suffocating. I heard hammering on the rubble, but when I looked around, I couldn’t see anything. I banged on the rubble with my hand so they’d hear me, until someone grabbed me and said, “Don’t be afraid. Don’t be afraid. We’re here to rescue you,” and then I passed out.

After that, I heard someone talking, and I felt him lifting me. He ran with me to the ambulance. When I opened my eyes, I found myself lying in a hospital bed. My whole body was wrapped in medical gauze, and the doctors were treating my head. I had terrible pain in my head and legs, and I suffered a lot.

Only a few days later, my aunt Amal told me that my mother and my siblings were no longer alive, that they had been killed. I’ve been crying over them all the time ever since.

On the day my father was also killed, my aunt Amal got a phone call and started crying. When I asked her, “What happened?” she told me that my uncles Muhammad and Ayman had been injured. She didn’t tell me that my father, Amin, was killed until later. I cried so much over him. My father was all I had left from my family, and all my hopes were pinned on him. He was my support. Now that he’s gone, I have no one except my aunt, Amal. I cry all the time and still can’t accept that I lost him too. I’m still in shock. There’s no one left from my family, and now only Aunt Amal takes care of me, looks after me, helps me, and comforts me. She is my family now.

I miss my family so much. A deep wound has opened in my soul, and nothing can heal it. After I also lost my father, my life was completely destroyed. He was all I had left.

* The testimonies were given to B’Tselem field researcher Muhammad Sabah on 19 October 2025

** On 5 November 2025, Amal managed to leave the Gaza Strip with Dima, Asmaa, and Hala and travel to Italy, where Dima is now receiving treatment. Asmaa’s eldest son, Baker, was denied entry to Jordan and had to return to the north of the Gaza Strip, where he now lives with what remains of Amal’s family.

Israel’s regime of apartheid and occupation is inextricably bound up in human rights violations. B’Tselem strives to end this regime, as that is the only way forward to a future in which human rights, democracy, liberty and equality are ensured to all people, both Palestinian and Israeli, living between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea.

Since the Hamas attack on 7 October 2023, Israel has acted in a coordinated and deliberate manner to destroy Palestinian society in the Gaza Strip, committing genocide against its residents. In light of Israel’s actions in Gaza, the public statements made by Israeli decision-makers, and the international community’s failure to take effective action, there is a serious risk that the Israeli regime will expand the genocide to other areas under its control—first and foremost, the West Bank.
B’Tselem calls on the Israeli public and the international community to use every tool available under international law to bring an immediate end to Israel’s genocide against the Palestinian people.
