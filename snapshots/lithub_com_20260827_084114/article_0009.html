“This Time and the Next”

By Noel Quiñones

The following is a story from The Best Short Stories 2026: The O. Henry Prize Winners, chosen by guest editor Tommy Orange and series editor Jenny Minton Quigley. Noel Quiñones is an Emmy Award–winning Nuyorican writer, educator, and speaker from the Bronx. Their work has been published in Poetry, Boston Review, Poem-a-day, and the anthology The BreakBeat Poets Vol. 4: LatiNEXT, as well as the Michigan Quarterly Review, for which they won the 2025 Jesmyn Ward Prize in Fiction. They have also received fellowships from CantoMundo, Lambda Literary, the Poetry Foundation, Tin House, and the Vermont Studio Center. A graduate of the University of Mississippi’s MFA program and founder of Project X, a Bronx-based spoken-word poetry organization, Noel is currently a poet in residence with the Chicago Poetry Center. Their debut poetry collection, Orange, is forthcoming in May 2026 from CavanKerry Press.

MCC New York
Metropolitan Correctional Center
150 Park Row
New York, NY 10007
Name: Francisco Santos
DIN: 14-C-2691

To: My Daughter Roselyn
From: Papi
Reason: Back soon
Date: 7/6/03

Rosie, you shouldnt have seen me like that. I hope your mom was able to pick you up from the station. I promise I will still take you to see that movie, what was it called again?

Remember this is your father were talking about. Whenver I say I will do something, I do it. Thats why Im in here, for you and your mom.

If you got questions make sure you ask me direct. I will be home soon. Tell your mom to call me.

Rosie, I love you and I miss you very much. I love you always, Papi xxxxxxx

Angelica Santos
593 Valentine Avenue
The Bronx, NY 10547
8/11/03

Papi, Grandma told me what happened. Why would they take you away for something someone else did? Ms. Grayson taught us about the Roman Empire last week and how they handled criminals. I hope this not what happens to you. I drew this for you, its us at soundview.

Love, roses

Dear Francisco,

Congratulations, you almost played me like you played Grandma. Thanks for waiting until the trailer visit to tell me the first thing you want to do when you get out is move to Florida with some woman I never met. What a fucking joke.

Nobody owes you a damn thing Francisco, not even the dude you shot. Not Grandma for trying her best to stop the infighting amongst us, not Tio for shutting down your dumbass prison break fantasies, and damn sure not Mami for not being a prison wife. Why do you think she makes me mail my letters from Grandma’s house? She doesn’t want you to know where she lives! She said in all this time you still haven’t changed.

I haven’t talked to Jenifer in years. You know why? Because I beat her ass when she started talking out the side of her mouth about you. I don’t like kid jokes anymore and I don’t just watch stupid action movies. Alfred Hitchcock is a famous director, he didn’t make American Psycho, he made Psycho. You would know that if you actually knew anything about movies. You would know what was happening with me if you weren’t stuck in jail. Maybe you would even think more about me if you weren’t so focused on starting a new family. And by the way, I lied during the visit, I was Batgirl for Halloween. Wrong again.

Do you know what I tell my friends at school about you? Nothing. I say that Jaime is my father.

You proud now? I’m a liar, just like you.

—Roselyn

PS Stop starting all your letters like a robot, WRITE like a normal fucking person.

Reason: The Butterfly Effect
Date: 5/22/11

Dear Roselyn,

Thank you and Tio for taking care of Grandma. I know she is hanging on and I hope she can hang on just a little longer for me to make it home, just six more months. She is strong, she can do it.

I need you to know that the week before I went in, my father called me. He heard I had a kid, and thought I could use a leg up. There was a dude in the neighborhood who owed him, said it was easy money. Your Grandpa was a ghost all my life. Nah, worse than a ghost because we remember them, like a memory pulled out your brain, gone. He wasn’t anything to me, but then suddenly he was, he was family.

I thought leaving on purpose and being forced to leave were different but they’re the same. They got the same effect.

Love, Papi

*

Roselyn Santos
101 N Merion Ave
Bryn Mawr, PA 19010
8/3/11

Dear Papi,

Tío told me that growing up he would say you could kill somebody and you’d still be her favorite. Now he knows for sure. Both of us told her to stop taking that long ass bus ride to see you when you canceled on her for Veronica, to stop calling you every day when all you did was talk shit about the rest of us, to stop putting you before the people who were actually in front of her. I hated you for the both of us because she wasn’t capable of it, the possibility never even crossed her mind.

I don’t know if that makes her the stupidest or the holiest woman on the planet. And I’m not stupid or holy but maybe I’m somewhere in between.

I’ve squeezed this penny in my fist until it bled but I can’t stop worrying about her. All I know is she wakes up every day in that hospital hoping you’re standing there.

You need to make it home, for her. Me and Tío will be there to pick you up.

Love, Roselyn
